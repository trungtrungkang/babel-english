# Product brief — Babel Online

## Pre-authoring review

Known requirements: classroom support, Speaking first, four roles, VI/EN and future skills. Assumptions: ages 8–11, the Explorers 1 label, durations and specific language. Awaiting Babel: first audience, unit outcome, materials, rubric and teacher time. Shopping is the detailed discussion unit; lessons 4–6 are expansion examples, not an approved curriculum.


Status: initial proposal for Vic and Châu to review. Created 29 September 2026.

## Vision

Help learners practise between Babel classroom lessons, progressing from repeating a model to speaking independently in a new situation. The long-term programme includes Speaking, Listening, Reading, Writing, Grammar, Vocabulary, Pronunciation, Homework, Review and Testing. Build Speaking first.

Success means better independent communication, useful evidence for teachers and clear progress for parents. Time spent in the app is not the primary outcome.

## People and needs

| Person | Main need | Evidence of value |
|---|---|---|
| Learner | Know what to do, feel safe speaking, try again | Starts independently and answers with fewer hints |
| Teacher | Set relevant practice and provide feedback efficiently | Identifies who needs help without excessive marking |
| Parent | Understand what their child is learning | Understands the report in about 30 seconds |
| Vic and Châu | Manage learning materials independently | Edit and publish without changing code |

## Experience principles

- One clear task on the home screen.
- Short, familiar situations built around Babel materials.
- A character acts as a conversation partner; open-ended AI chat is unnecessary for the first trial.
- Gradually remove support and change details to test transfer beyond memorisation.
- Celebrate effort and improvement, without public leaderboards.
- Treat age and language level separately: older beginners need age-appropriate presentation.

## Working assumptions

The demo targets ages 8–11. “Explorers 1” is a sample label, not an agreed mapping to Babel’s curriculum. The proposed lesson length is 5–8 minutes, not a measured result. Existing Babel learners are the first pilot audience. Adults should provide demonstration recordings; trials with children need appropriate consent and data handling. Babel will supply representative materials before bulk production.

## Decisions after the demonstration

- Which age group, level and classes should go first?
- Which classroom lesson does each online mission reinforce, and how often?
- What counts as independent speaking at each level?
- Which videos, audio, transcripts and usage rights are available?
- How much weekly teacher review time is feasible?
- Who approves pedagogy and who manages content?
- Which devices and connections do families use?
- What budget, team and timing are available?

## Research context

These references inform design; they do not establish Babel Online’s effectiveness.

- [EEF: Oral language interventions](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/oral-language-interventions): purposeful interaction integrated with the curriculum. Much of this evidence concerns literacy, not app-based speaking gains.
- [Retrieval Practice: Spacing](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/spacing): active recall and spaced review inform the proposed review design.
- [Recognition of non-native child speech](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16710): validate speech recognition in the actual learner context.
- [UNICEF: AI and children](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children): children’s interests, privacy and transparency should guide AI decisions.
